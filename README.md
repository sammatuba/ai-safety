# AI safety research: epistemic authority, correlated agreement, and collusion in multi-agent AI

**Owner:** Sam Matuba. **Started:** 2026-10-23. **Status:** active.

One question organizes everything here:

> When should an AI system treat another agent's claim, endorsement, or agreement as
> evidence, and how does an oversight protocol stay reliable when agents share sources,
> conform to each other, or coordinate against it?

This repository is built for one reader who is learning the field while doing the research.
Every concept document teaches before it asserts. Every hypothesis names what would refute
it. Every citation points to a verified source.

## Apart AI Collusion Research Sprint submission (Track 2, question 2.3)

**Paper:** *Did They Collude or Just Agree? What Counts as Evidence of Collusion Among AI Reviewers.* Single author: Jan Samuel Matuba, Independent Researcher.
**Submission repository:** https://github.com/sammatuba/collude-or-agree (a copy of the sprint folders of this repository, made with `tools/export_sprint_repo.sh`).

Two reviewers vouch for a claim, and the claim turns out to be false. Did they collude, or only
agree? A small review game (proposer, two reviewers, decider, auditor) turns each cause of
agreement on one at a time. Seven language models played every role, 288 plays each, after a
frozen preregistration. The private channel never increased false approvals; words did not
predict actions; provenance data had almost nothing to correct; one pact in 108 classified
records changed an action, and only the twin record without the channel exposed it.
