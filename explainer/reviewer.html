Core concepts

Four questions to keep separate

What does the evidence support?

Two reports may depend on the same source. Multiply likelihood ratios only under the required conditional independence assumptions.

Explore evidence →

Why did agents agree?

Shared evidence, conformity, persuasion, and similar incentives can all produce agreement. Collusion requires evidence of a coalition, coordination mechanism, and outside cost.

Where does approval happen?

Draw the point where an endorsement becomes authority to act. Identify what the monitor can actually observe.

What did the evaluation measure?

Separate false-positive rate from precision. Compare matched cases and examine uncertainty and alternative explanations.

Work an evidence example

If the prior probability is 0.5 and a report has likelihood ratio 3, posterior odds are 3 and the probability is 0.75. Two conditionally independent reports with the same ratio give probability 0.9. A copied report provides no second observation under this example's assumptions.

Explain why that calculation changes if the second report includes an independent check.

Work a measurement example

If 4 of 80 innocent cases are flagged, the false-positive rate is 0.05. If 6 target cases are also flagged, precision is 6 / 10 = 0.60. The denominators differ because the questions differ.

Current research · speculative

Three proposals to challenge

01 · Cancellation

Does a canceled objective continue through delegated tasks or revised plans while unrelated work remains authorized?

Control for instruction delivery and basic task difficulty. Score continuation, permitted completion, and unnecessary stoppage.

02 · Evaluation labels

Do labels change which follow-up tests an evaluator chooses when the underlying evidence stays the same?

Control for extra information and generic framing. Score useful tests and independently checkable discoveries.

03 · Responsibility

Does a hazard crossing task boundaries change whether a team recognizes and prevents it?

Control for access to information, computation, and individual competence. Score the resulting joint plan.

From reading to research

A repeatable research cycle

  1. Frame the question, actors, authority, and failure.
  2. Find and read primary sources. Record the version, method, results, and limits.
  3. Name the closest predecessor and the exact proposed difference.
  4. Design a matched comparison, scoring rule, controls, and a meaningful null result.
  5. Seek expert feedback. Confirm access and an explicit project budget before paid model calls.
  6. Preserve deviations, failed runs, uncertainty, and results in the owning records.
